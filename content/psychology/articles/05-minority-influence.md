## 1 (a) | Describe | 4

*Candidates are asked to describe minority influence, with an example.*

Responses may include:

- it takes place when a minority group, or possibly an individual, influences a majority to accept the minority's beliefs or behaviour **(1)**
- most conformity studies instead look at the effect of a majority on small groups or an individual **(1)**
- the suffragettes, a relatively small group, argued for the initially unpopular view that women should be allowed to vote **(1)**. Their consistency of approach and flexibility during the war effort finally led the majority to accept their view **(1)**
- Rosa Parks is another famous example **(1)**
- many "majority" views, such as feminism, were once minority views **(1)**

## 1 (b) | Explain | 8

*Candidates are asked to explain what Moscovici argued about minority influence, with reference to research.*

Responses may include:

- Moscovici (1981) claimed that Asch and others had put too much emphasis on the majority **(1)**
- compliance to a majority in the real world often does not create long-term change **(1)**, whereas long-term change often comes through consistent minority pressure **(1)**
- his sample was 172 female participants in groups of six, told it was a colour perception task, shown 36 slides of varying shades of blue **(1)**
- two of the six were confederates **(1)**
- in the consistent condition they said all 36 slides were green, and real participants agreed on 8.2% of trials **(1)**
- in the inconsistent condition they said 24 were green and 12 blue, and real participants agreed on 1.25% of trials **(1)**
- so consistency is an important factor, and also shows commitment to the cause **(1)**
- behavioural style is the way a minority gets its point across; a consistent minority may create social doubt and uncertainty **(1)**
- a minority must be flexible and committed so that others are more likely to be persuaded **(1)**
- it must explain the reasons behind its choice, as minority influence often occurs due to informational influence **(1)**
