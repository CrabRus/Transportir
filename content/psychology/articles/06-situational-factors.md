## 1 | Explain | 6

*Candidates are asked to explain situational factors affecting conformity.*

Where candidates only provide descriptions of situational factors, **a maximum of 3 marks** can be awarded.

Where only one situational factor is explained, award **a maximum of 4 marks**.

Reasons we conform can be situational (varying from one situation to another), individual or cultural.

Responses may include:

**Group size**

- as the majority grows, conformity increases, but only up to a certain point **(1)**
- Asch found almost no conformity with 1 confederate, 12.8% with 2 and 33.3% with 3 **(1)**. More confederates made only a slight difference **(1)**

**Social support**

- if we have support in a group, we may be less likely to conform **(1)**, because the majority is no longer unanimous **(1)**
- when one confederate acted as an ally, seated fourth and answering before the true participant, conformity fell sharply to 5.5% **(1)**

**Secrecy of response**

- if we can avoid responding in front of other people, compliance is less likely to occur **(1)**, because this eliminates the fear of ridicule **(1)**
- in a variation, participants told they were late wrote their answers privately, and conformity fell **(1)**

**Similarity of group**

- in real life we are more likely to be influenced by friends and colleagues than by strangers, and where a group know one another conformity may be even higher **(1)**
- if participants feel they share characteristics with the majority, such as the same class, they are more likely to experience normative influence **(1)**

**Task difficulty**

- we show higher levels of conformity on a difficult task **(1)**, because we may look to others for guidance when we lack confidence in our own judgements **(1)**
- in an ambiguous situation we may look to others for a reasonable answer, as shown by Jenness and Sherif **(1)**
- in another variation, Asch put the line lengths closer together, making judging them harder **(1)**
