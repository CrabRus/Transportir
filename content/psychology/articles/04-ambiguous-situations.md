## 1 (a) | Explain | 8

*Candidates are asked to explain one conformity study involving an ambiguous situation.*

Candidates must link the study to informational influence for full marks.

Responses may include:

**Jenness (1932)**

- the aim was to investigate conformity and informational influence, in a laboratory experiment **(1)**
- the ambiguous situation was a glass bottle filled with 811 white beans **(1)**
- 101 Psychology students individually estimated how many beans the bottle contained **(1)**
- they were put into groups of three and asked to agree on a group estimate **(1)**
- they were interviewed individually again and asked if they would like to change their estimates **(1)**
- almost all changed their individual guesses to be closer to the group estimate **(1)**
- this was informational influence: participants were unsure of the answer so looked to the group for assistance **(1)**

**Sherif (1935)**

- Sherif investigated responses to an ambiguous stimulus using the autokinetic effect, estimating how far a light appeared to move **(1)**
- participants worked with three others who had given quite different estimates of movement **(1)**
- after discussion, individual answers changed and became quite similar **(1)**
- the task was tricky to judge, so participants relied on others' answers: informational influence **(1)**

## 1 (b) | Analyse | 4

*Candidates are asked to compare these studies with Asch's.*

Points of analysis may include:

- Asch used an easy task with an obvious answer, whereas Jenness and Sherif used ambiguous tasks with no clear answer **(1)**
- this means Asch mainly shows normative influence, whereas Jenness and Sherif mainly show informational influence **(1)**
- in Asch's study people conformed to fit in and avoid ridicule **(1)**, whereas here they were unsure and looked to others for the answer **(1)**
