## 1 (a) | Describe | 8

*Candidates are asked to describe Asch's (1951) study of conformity.*

Responses may include:

- the aim was to see whether people would conform to others' incorrect estimates when the task was easy and the correct answer obvious **(1)**, so he was exploring normative influence **(1)**
- fifty male American participants were told it was an experiment into visual perception **(1)**
- everyone else in the group was an actor (a confederate) whose responses had been prepared in advance **(1)**
- the group saw 18 cards and matched a line to one of three comparison lines **(1)**
- confederates gave the same wrong answer on 12 of the 18, called the critical trials **(1)**
- the true participant was always last or second last to answer **(1)**
- there was a control group of 37 participants **(1)**
- in the control group only 1% of answers were incorrect **(1)**
- there was a mean of 3.84 errors out of 12 critical trials, against 0.08 in the control group **(1)**
- 25% of participants did not conform at all **(1)**, and 75% gave a wrong answer at least once **(1)**
- even when correct, participants often appeared withdrawn or embarrassed **(1)**

## 1 (b) | Explain | 6

*Candidates are asked to explain the conclusions drawn from Asch's study.*

Responses may include:

- people do not wish to be ridiculed and excluded by a majority **(1)**, so conformity took place through normative influence, the desire to fit in **(1)**
- most participants said afterwards that they did not really believe their conforming answers **(1)**, but went along for fear of being ridiculed or thought "peculiar" **(1)**
- a few really did believe the group's answers were correct **(1)**, showing informational influence, as they believed the group was better informed **(1)**
- reasons given included genuinely doubting their own judgement **(1)**, not wanting to "upset the experiment", and not wanting to appear odd **(1)**

## 1 (c) | Evaluate | 6

*Candidates are asked to evaluate Asch's study of conformity.*

Where candidates provide only generic evaluations, **a maximum of half marks** can be awarded.

Responses may include strengths, weaknesses, or both:

- it was a powerful demonstration of group influence **(1)**, as even strangers can make people respond very differently to how they would when alone **(1)**
- it was hugely influential **(1)**, and many other studies, such as Mori and Arai, have used a similar task **(1)**
- it lacked ecological validity as it was an artificial lab experiment **(1)**. The task lacked mundane realism, as real-life pressure to conform is not so clear-cut **(1)**
- 1950s America, during the Cold War, encouraged conformity **(1)**, so the findings can't necessarily be generalised to other places and eras; it has been called "a child of its time" **(1)**
- Perrin and Spencer (1981) found much lower conformity in British engineering students **(1)**, suggesting that findings depended on interpretation of the situation and on cultural norms **(1)**
- the sample was only 50 males **(1)**, so results cannot be generalised to females **(1)**
