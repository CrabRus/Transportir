## 1 (a) | Explain | 8

*Candidates are asked to explain the two motivations to conform (Deutsch and Gerard, 1955), with reference to studies.*

Where candidates only describe the motivations, **a maximum of 4 marks** can be awarded.

Candidates must refer to both motivations for full marks. They may occur separately or at once.

Responses may include:

**Normative social influence**

- a person is not in doubt but is influenced by social norms **(1)**. The pressure comes from the group, based on a need to be liked and accepted **(1)**
- it is the dominant form when we are concerned about making a good impression in front of a group **(1)**
- we may disagree secretly but verbally adopt the group stance so that we seem like a team player rather than a deviant **(1)**
- it often comes from real or imagined peer pressure; reasons include fear of rejection, wanting approval, being identified as part of a group and showing loyalty **(1)**
- in Asch's study participants conformed so as not to be ridiculed when the answer was obvious **(1)**. Mori and Arai also illustrate this **(1)**
- it is strongly linked to compliance, where people change their answers publicly but maintain their private beliefs **(1)**

**Informational social influence**

- uncertainty leads a person to adopt the behaviour of others, because they don't know what to do and want to be correct **(1)**
- it often occurs in situations of high uncertainty and ambiguity, or in an unfamiliar situation **(1)**
- the actions of others inform us of the customs and accepted practices in a situation **(1)**
- it is concerned with the fear of looking unintelligent and believing others know better, especially if the task is difficult **(1)**
- in Jenness' study participants were unsure, so looked to others' guesses and changed their answer to align with the group **(1)**. Sherif also illustrates this **(1)**
- it is strongly linked to internalisation, as people change their view publicly and privately because they think others are correct **(1)**
