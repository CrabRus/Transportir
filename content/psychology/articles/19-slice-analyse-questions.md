## 1 (a) | Describe | 5

*Candidates are asked to describe the SLICE structure. Each letter will be a separate paragraph.*

- S, Summary: description of the study (aim, method, results) **(1)**
- L, Links: to concepts and theories **(1)**
- I, Implications: how the findings apply to real life and what conclusions we can draw **(1)**
- C, Compare: with other similar studies **(1)**
- E, Evaluation of the evidence **(1)**

## 1 (b) | Explain | 4

*Candidates are asked to explain how marks are allocated in an "Analyse" question.*

Responses may include:

- summary is rewarded 25% of the overall mark, as descriptive marks **(1)**
- evaluation is rewarded 25%, as evaluative marks **(1)**
- links, implications and compare are analysis marks, and at least half of the marks must come from analysis **(1)**
- in a 12-mark question this is summary 3, evaluation 3 and analysis 6 **(1)**
- to gain analysis marks, explain the "so what" of your point, using starters such as "This links to...", "An implication of this is...", "In contrast to this..." or "This is supported by..." **(1)**
- do not overload the summary, as you risk not having enough time for analysis **(1)**

## 1 (c) | Analyse | 12

*Candidates are asked to analyse Milgram's study of obedience.*

Award **a maximum of 3 marks** for descriptions of the study.

Award **a maximum of 3 marks** for evaluation of the study.

Candidates can gain the full **12 marks** for analysis.

Points of analysis may include:

- it was concluded that when given orders by a figure of authority, we are likely to obey **(1)**
- people are in an agentic state when they follow orders given by an authority figure **(1)**
- participants who did not obey took personal responsibility, and so were in an autonomous state **(1)**
- higher obedience with teacher and learner in different rooms is because they are protected from seeing the effects on the learner **(1)**
- when our feeling of personal responsibility increases, obedience decreases, as seen when teachers were in close proximity to learners **(1)**
- the results help us to understand German soldiers' behaviour in the Second World War, as most said they were following orders **(1)**
- Milgram wanted to test the general public, and used males to mimic those involved in the Nazi atrocities **(1)**
- participants were deceived so informed consent was not obtained, though knowing the true purpose may have reduced participant numbers and validity **(1)**
- the experimenter's language reinforced authority and the belief that they could not leave, leading to signs of stress **(1)**
- Milgram's lab study has greater reliability and Bickman's field study greater ecological validity **(1)**, and both found that uniform played a part in obedience **(1)**
- Hofling found 95% obedience compared to Milgram's 65%, showing that people are very unwilling to question supposed authority **(1)**

## 2 | Analyse | 4

*Candidates are asked what to include when analysing Mori and Arai's study. The full 12-mark question is in the Mori and Arai article.*

Points of analysis may include:

- comparison with Asch, Perrin and Spencer, Zimbardo, Eagly and Carli, Jenness, or Sherif **(1)**
- links to theories and factors: normative social influence, gender, similarity of group **(1)**
- real-life application, for example educating about peer pressure **(1)**
- ethics: participants were not told the true aim **(1)**
