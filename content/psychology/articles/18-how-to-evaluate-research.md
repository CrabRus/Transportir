## 1 (a) | Describe | 6

*Candidates are asked to describe the criteria used to evaluate research.*

Responses may include:

- ecological validity: can the findings be applied to the real world? Was it a real-life task? **(1)**
- research method: carried out in a lab or in the field? This shows reliability **(1)**
- sampling size and method: is it representative of the whole population? **(1)**
- date: can the findings be applied to behaviour today? **(1)**
- cultural relativism: can the findings be applied to different cultures? **(1)**
- ethics: can findings be valid if guidelines have been broken? **(1)**

## 1 (b) | Evaluate | 6

*Candidates are asked to evaluate a study, using this structure: "A strength is... This is a strength because..." or "A weakness is... This is a weakness because...".*

Where candidates provide only generic evaluations, **a maximum of half marks** can be awarded. Use evaluative language: generalise, reliable, applicable, valid, ethics.

Responses may include:

- Milgram's research was unethical as participants thought it was a study about memory, not obedience **(1)**. Participants were deceived and could have acted abnormally as a result, so results are unreliable **(1)**
- Mori and Arai used both males and females **(1)**, so the results can be generalised to both **(1)**
- Mori and Arai only used participants from Japan **(1)**. Japan is a collectivist culture and conformity levels will differ from individualist cultures, so it is not generalisable **(1)**
- being specific about a lab study: researchers controlled things like how participants viewed lines **(1)**, therefore making it valid and reliable **(1)**

## 1 (c) | Explain | 4

*Candidates are asked to explain a factor with reference to a scenario, using Point, Explain, Evidence, Link to scenario.*

Candidates must refer to the scenario to gain full marks. Always read the scenario fully.

Responses may include:

- point: give the factor and its direction, for example location affects obedience, and less prestigious places lower it **(1)**
- explain: why it causes obedience or conformity to increase or decrease **(1)**
- evidence: Milgram's run-down office variation, where obedience dropped from 65% to 47.5% **(1)**
- link to the scenario: Susan obeys her supervisor in the work office, as she sees it as a more credible location than the college **(1)**
