## 1 (a) | Explain | 8

*Candidates are asked to explain autonomous and agentic levels of behaviour.*

Where candidates only describe the two states, **a maximum of 4 marks** can be awarded.

Responses may include:

- in the autonomous state, people direct their own actions and take responsibility for the results **(1)**, acting according to their own values and beliefs **(1)**, so obedience is lower **(1)**
- in the agentic state, people allow others to direct their actions and pass off responsibility to the person giving the orders **(1)**, so obedience is higher **(1)**
- the move from autonomous to agentic is the agentic shift **(1)**, after which the individual no longer feels responsible and can blame those in higher authority **(1)**
- entering the agentic state requires a perceived legitimate authority **(1)**, and a belief that the authority will accept responsibility **(1)**
- when another confederate gave the shocks, obedience rose to 92% **(1)**, as participants shifted responsibility and felt less responsible **(1)**
- participants who did not obey took personal responsibility, so were in an autonomous state **(1)**; 35% did not go to 450 volts **(1)**

## 1 (b) | Explain | 6

*Candidates are asked to explain why people obey with reference to socialisation.*

Responses may include:

- socialisation is the effect of childhood experiences on making us behave according to social norms **(1)**
- society has taught us to respect and obey authority, such as parents and teachers **(1)**, and this is one reason we enter the agentic state **(1)**
- childhood is when we learn society's rules, including who is considered an authority figure **(1)**
- it results from collective social pressures from family, teachers and peers **(1)**, and we take these beliefs into adulthood **(1)**
- Weber's sources of authority are all part of our socialisation **(1)**
- Anderson and Zimbardo (1984) describe the "good child" as cooperative and polite, one who does not make trouble **(1)**

## 1 (c) | Explain | 5

*Candidates are asked to explain how authoritarian parenting affects obedience.*

Responses may include:

- parenting is one of the biggest influences on socialisation **(1)**
- authoritarian parents make obedience and respect for authority the top priority **(1)**, and rules should be obeyed and not discussed **(1)**
- their children are more likely to obey and less likely to think for themselves or question consequences **(1)**
- democratic parents encourage children to think for themselves and negotiate reasonable boundaries **(1)**, so these children are less likely to obey **(1)**
- however, it is hard to put parents in a particular category **(1)**, and as we grow older peers become more influential **(1)**

## 1 (d) | Describe | 8

*Candidates are asked to state the direction in which each factor changes obedience.*

- wearing a uniform increases obedience **(1)**
- being in the agentic state increases obedience **(1)**
- being in the autonomous state decreases obedience **(1)**
- close proximity to a "victim" decreases obedience **(1)**
- close proximity to an authority figure increases obedience **(1)**
- democratic parents decrease obedience in later life **(1)**
- authoritarian parents increase obedience in later life **(1)**
- socialisation increases obedience **(1)**
