## 1 (a) | Describe | 6

*Candidates are asked to describe the Stanford Prison Experiment.*

Responses may include:

- Zimbardo wanted to assess the reason for growing unrest in US prisons in the 1960s **(1)**
- normal people took the role of prisoner or guard, to explore if they would conform to these roles **(1)**
- males volunteered for a study of "prison life" and were paid $15 a day **(1)**
- 10 "prisoners" were unexpectedly "arrested" at home and put through a rigorous search on arrival **(1)**
- guards had a uniform, clubs, whistles and handcuffs, and only referred to prisoners by number **(1)**
- it was intended to last two weeks but was cut short after six days **(1)**
- before the sixth day five prisoners had been released as a result of extreme depression **(1)**
- guards woke prisoners in the night, locked them in closets and made them clean toilets with bare hands **(1)**
- guards volunteered to work overtime, and participants at times appeared to forget they were only acting **(1)**

## 1 (b) | Explain | 4

*Candidates are asked to explain what the study suggests about why people conform.*

Responses may include:

- if participants had not conformed, behaviour could be explained by personality, an individual factor **(1)**
- if they did conform, it could be explained by environment, a social factor **(1)**
- both guards and prisoners conformed to their social roles as a result of normative social influence **(1)**
- situational factors were the most important: ordinary students could easily become brutal guards in the right setting **(1)**
