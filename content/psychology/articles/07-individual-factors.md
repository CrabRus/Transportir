> **Note on the materials.** The sources agree teenagers are most affected, but differ on the detail. The booklet says conformity is unchanged from 10 to 14 and then rises up to 18. Lesson 6 says it is highest from 10 to 14 and decreases from 14 to 18, as the ability to dissent rises. Check with your teacher.

## 1 | Explain | 6

*Candidates are asked to explain two individual factors that affect conformity.*

Where only one factor is explained, award **a maximum of 4 marks**. No marks for explanations of conformity alone.

Responses may include:

**Age**

- teenagers are the most likely to be affected by conformity (Steinberg and Monahan, 2007) **(1)**
- this is largely attributed to high levels of peer influence and pressure in adolescence (Brown, 2004) **(1)**
- age can also be a situational factor, as you may be more likely to conform to your teacher than to an 8 year old **(1)**

**Gender**

- women tend to conform more than men (Mori and Arai, 2010) **(1)**
- this may be because women tend to take a communal role and promote harmony in a group, while men are more comfortable maintaining independence (Eagly and Carli, 1987) **(1)**
- in Mori and Arai females conformed on 4.41 critical trials whereas men did not conform **(1)**, and this is likely to be caused by normative social influence **(1)**
- however, these studies are often outdated or rely on culture **(1)**

**Thought processes**

- people interpret situations differently: in Asch's study some genuinely doubted their judgement whilst others knew they were right but followed the crowd **(1)**
- some may be less likely to conform if they hold a strong view **(1)**. Hornsey et al (2003) found that students with strong beliefs about the law and recognition of gay couples were publicly and privately less likely to conform **(1)**
- there is some evidence that we are more likely to conform in a good mood than a bad mood (Tong et al., 2007) **(1)**
- with fear-then-relief, someone is made afraid and the fear is then relieved, making them more likely to do what you want **(1)**

**Personality**

- people with higher self-esteem were less likely to conform (Santee and Maslach, 1982) **(1)**, as they tend not to be influenced by how others evaluate them **(1)**
- Asch suggested people low in self-esteem are more likely to conform as they are more likely to fear rejection **(1)**
- people with a greater need for liking from others are more likely to conform **(1)**
- people who value control are less likely to conform **(1)**. Burger's participants rated cartoons in the presence of confederates, and those high in desire for personal control were less likely to match them **(1)**
