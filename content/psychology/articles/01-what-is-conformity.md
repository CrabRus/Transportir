## 1 (a) | Describe | 4

*Candidates are asked to describe what is meant by conformity.*

Responses may include:

- conformity is a type of social influence involving a change in belief or behaviour in order to fit in with a group **(1)**
- this change is in response to real (the physical presence of others) or imagined (the pressure of social norms and expectations) group pressure **(1)**
- it can be defined as "yielding to group pressures" (Crutchfield, 1955) and is also known as majority influence **(1)**
- it usually results from pressure from peers or groups of strangers, and media pressure may also have a strong influence **(1)**
- the pressure is typically unspoken: we respond to what others are doing and change our behaviour to fit in **(1)**
- examples are the way we dress, smoking to "fit in", liking music because friends do, or acting differently at work than at home **(1)**

## 1 (b) | Explain | 6

*Candidates are asked to explain three types of conformity, using examples.*

Where candidates only describe the types of conformity, **a maximum of 3 marks** can be awarded.

Candidates must refer to all three types (Kelman, 1958) for full marks.

Responses may include:

- compliance is conforming publicly whilst privately maintaining your own view **(1)**. It is the shallowest level, for example laughing at a joke that you do not find funny **(1)**
- identification is conforming whilst in the group because the person identifies with it and feels a sense of group membership **(1)**. The change is often temporary, for example recycling with flatmates but not during the holidays **(1)**
- internalisation is conforming publicly and privately, even when no longer a member of the group **(1)**. It is the deepest level, for example staying vegetarian after leaving the job where you became one **(1)**

| Type | Public | Private | How long it lasts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compliance | Agrees | Disagrees | Only while the group can see |
| Identification | Agrees | Agrees while in the group | Often temporary |
| Internalisation | Agrees | Agrees | Permanent, even after leaving the group |
