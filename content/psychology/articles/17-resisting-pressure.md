## 1 | Explain | 10

*Candidates are asked to explain strategies for resisting social pressure.*

Responses may include:

**Feeling of responsibility**

- taking responsibility is led by empathy and guilt **(1)**
- people can be reminded that they, not the authorities, are responsible for harm, and this really reduces the tendency to obey **(1)**
- those who refused to go to 450V often said they felt responsible for the learner, so were in an autonomous state **(1)**

**Disobedient role models and social support**

- seeing what happens when disobedience takes place makes it easier for individuals to follow on **(1)**; Rosa Parks is a good example **(1)**
- whistle blowers expose damaging practices of businesses and governments, and laws protect them from prosecution **(1)**

**Questioning motives**

- people who question the expertise and motives of authority figures find it easier to resist **(1)**, considering if a command serves socially beneficial goals or the authority's selfish gains **(1)**

**Moral reasoning: Kohlberg (1969)**

- those at an advanced stage of moral reasoning were more likely to disobey **(1)**
- at the pre-conventional level people judge morality by direct consequences **(1)**; at the conventional level they understand norms needed for a stable society **(1)**; at the post-conventional level they base morals on humanitarian principles **(1)**
- most of Milgram's participants who resisted reasoned at a higher stage of morality **(1)**

**Education**

- education raises awareness of cults and manipulators **(1)**, and teaches skills such as the broken record technique ("I don't want to join") **(1)**
- it also encourages confidence and democratic parenting over authoritarian parenting **(1)**
