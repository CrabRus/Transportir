> **Note on the materials.** The booklet rounds the location figure to 48%; the lesson and marked answers use 47.5%. The phone variation is 21% in most sources, 20.5% in one marking instruction.

## 1 | Explain | 6

*Candidates are asked to explain situational factors affecting obedience.*

Where candidates only provide descriptions of situational factors, **a maximum of 3 marks** can be awarded.

Where only one situational factor is explained, award **a maximum of 4 marks**.

Milgram carried out 18 variations, altering the situation (IV) to see how this affected obedience (DV).

| Variation | Factor | Obedience (baseline 65%) |
|---|---|---|
| Run-down office block instead of Yale | Location | 47.5% |
| Experimenter in normal clothes | Uniform | 20% |
| Instructions given over the phone | Proximity to authority | 21% |
| Experimenter left the room | Presence of authority | 20% |
| Teacher and learner in the same room | Proximity to victim | 40% |
| Teacher forced learner's hand onto plate | Proximity to victim | 30% |
| Another confederate gave the shocks | Shifted responsibility | 92% |

Responses may include:

- location: prestigious locations convey importance and credibility, which increases obedience **(1)**. Obedience dropped from 65% at Yale to 47.5% in a run-down building **(1)**
- uniform: a uniform conveys a sense of legitimate authority, so we are more likely to obey **(1)**. When the person giving orders wore everyday clothes, obedience dropped to 20% **(1)**. Bickman supports this **(1)**
- presence of authority: when the authority figure is removed, obedience falls **(1)**. When the experimenter left after initial instructions, it dropped to 20% **(1)**
- proximity to authority: the closer the authority figure, the more pressure we feel and the more likely we are to obey **(1)**. Over the phone, obedience dropped to 21% **(1)**
- proximity to victim: the closer we are and can see someone suffer, the less likely we are to obey **(1)**. The wall acted as a buffer, making it easier to deliver a shock **(1)**
- obedience dropped to 40% in the same room **(1)** and to 30% when holding the hand on the plate, which is still surprisingly high, so proximity is not the only factor **(1)**
- Milgram argued that in particular circumstances everyone will obey **(1)**, which goes against the idea that the Nazis carried out their crimes because of their personality **(1)**
